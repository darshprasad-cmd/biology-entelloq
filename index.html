The Mission

We believe every student is one missing mechanism away from understanding. Biology Entelloq shows it — running, and in their hands.

Why biology goes flat

Biology isn't memorisation. It's been taught that way.

Living processes are drawn as still diagrams. The dissection most syllabuses still examine is one most students never actually perform. And a mechanism you cannot run is a mechanism you can only recite.

FAILURE_01

Life, drawn as a still picture

A textbook cardiac cycle is four arrows on a page. Nothing opens, nothing closes, nothing has a pressure.

→ EntelloqEvery process is a running model. Change the heart rate and watch diastole shorten faster than systole does.
FAILURE_02

The practical nobody performs

Dissection is examined far more often than it is done — banned, unaffordable, or simply never timetabled.

→ EntelloqA dissection theatre with five specimens, opened with your own hands through the webcam. No glassware, no risk, no cleanup.
FAILURE_03

Answers without mechanism

You can name the limiting factors of photosynthesis and still not know why more light sometimes changes nothing at all.

→ EntelloqStarve the CO₂, then crank the light. The rate refuses to move — and the reason stops being a sentence you memorised.

What is actually in it

Not a textbook with a chatbot bolted on. Purpose-built engines, each one a thing you operate rather than read.

The Dissection Lab

Five specimens — frog, mammalian heart, earthworm, bony fish, cockroach — on a table you light, pin and open. Scalpel, forceps, probe and pins, with tissue that resists and separates the way tissue does. Optional webcam hand tracking makes your hands the instruments: pinch to grip, and a turn of the wrist changes tool. The video never leaves your machine.

Get in touch
Questions

The things people actually ask.

Does it really work offline?

Yes, and that is not a slogan. Each page is a single self-contained HTML file with its dependencies inlined — including three.js for the 3D. Save it and open it on a machine that has never seen the internet and the dissection theatre still runs. The two exceptions are the webcam hand-tracking model, which is downloaded from Google's servers the first time you switch hand tracking on, and Google sign-in, which obviously needs Google.

Do I need an account?

No. Everything works signed out. Signing in with Google gives you a name and a picture and nothing else — your work is written to this browser either way, and signing out does not remove it.

Where does my webcam video go?

Nowhere. The hand tracking runs entirely inside your browser: each frame is read, turned into twenty-one hand landmarks, and discarded. No video is recorded, stored or uploaded, and there is no server here to upload it to.

Which syllabuses does it cover?

The content is built around NEET, CBSE, AP Biology, IB and Olympiad biology. The dissection specimens are the ones those practicals actually examine — frog, mammalian heart, earthworm, bony fish and cockroach.

Is any of it made up?

The models are checked before they ship. The microscope's cells are drawn to its own printed scale bar, the cardiac pressure traces are continuous across the whole beat and put the dicrotic notch where it belongs, and the predator–prey bench integrates the actual Lotka–Volterra equations rather than drawing a decorative wave. Where something is a simplification, it is a simplification of the real thing.

What does it cost?

Nothing. There is no paid tier, no trial and no card. If that changes it will not change retroactively for anything you already have.
