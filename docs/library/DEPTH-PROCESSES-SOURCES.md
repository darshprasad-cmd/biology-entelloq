# Process and systems depth lessons

Editorial review date: 2026-09-30.

`src/library/depth-processes.js` supplies 36 original lessons, covering the existing topic indices 37–72 (light-dependent reactions through CRISPR). Each lesson includes three learning objectives, three mechanism stages, a misconception correction, an evidence-interpretation task, a transfer problem and one or two topic-specific source links. The lesson explanations, questions, tables and solutions were authored for this product; no publisher figures or exercises were reproduced.

## Evidence and references

The source objects beside each lesson are the student-facing reference record. Sources were consulted in grouped checks of bioenergetics and molecular biology, physiology and plant transport, ecology, and biotechnology. References include the relevant original OpenStax textbook pages, NIH/NCBI educational books, NHGRI definitions and explanatory pages, NASA carbon-cycle explanations, and CDC antimicrobial-resistance material.

Additional references used for areas where a simplified textbook account could conceal an important qualification:

- [NCBI Bookshelf: Chloroplasts and photosynthesis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26819/) — cyclic electron flow and ATP/NADPH coupling.
- [NCBI Bookshelf: Energy conversion](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK21063/) — electrochemical gradients and ATP synthesis.
- [Plant Physiology: Guard-cell transport and stomatal dynamics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5462021/) and [Annual Review of Plant Biology: Guard-cell signaling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3056615/) — guard-cell ion transport, turgor and integrated regulation. These are scholarly reviews, not newly generated experimental evidence.
- [NCBI Bookshelf: Differential gene expression](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10061/) — cell identity through gene regulation, with explicit exceptions to universal genomic equivalence.
- [NIH: Stem cell basics](https://stemcells.nih.gov/info/basics/stc-basics) and [NIGMS: What are stem cells?](https://www.nigms.nih.gov/biobeat/2024/11/what-are-stem-cells) — self-renewal versus differentiation potential.
- [NHGRI: Genome-editing mechanisms](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/policy-issues/Genome-Editing/How-genome-editing-works) — targeting versus the resulting sequence change.

## Assumptions that must stay visible

- Investigation tables are explicitly fictional/illustrative unless they are theoretical accounting exercises. They are not measured data, patient records, clinical thresholds, organism-specific performance predictions or wet-laboratory protocols.
- Theoretical tables cover Calvin-cycle carbon accounting, strand-label inheritance and logistic-model rates. PCR doubling is explicitly idealized, and gel calibration is a stipulated logarithmic model.
- Relative outputs have only the comparison meaning stated in their lesson. They must not be converted into universal biochemical stoichiometries.
- Physiological quantities are model inputs. Blood content is distinguished from saturation and delivery; cardiac pressure ordering does not by itself identify contraction versus relaxation; refractory behavior at one stimulus strength does not prove absolute refractoriness.
- The immune-memory table includes both repeated-antigen and matched unrelated-antigen controls. An antibody signal is not equated with infection prevention, clinical protection or all components of immunity.
- Population fractions are separated from absolute counts. This matters for allele-frequency drift, resistance selection, source-to-sink allocation and editing-outcome comparisons.
- Biotechnology lessons address conceptual mechanisms and interpretation. They contain no sequence designs, experimental operating conditions, pathogen engineering or clinical decision instructions.
